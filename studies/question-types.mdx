Versive offers 20+ question types organized into four groups: **Open response**, **Choices & sorting**, **Ratings & feedback**, and **Other**. Most structured question types have a **Required** toggle (see [Required and request response](#required-and-request-response)), and most support media attachments — see [Question media](/studies/question-media).

<Callout icon="info-circle" color="#71717a">
**Surveys** (the no-AI engine) hide the conversational types (AI Question and Exploratory) and use structured types only.
</Callout>

## Several questions on one page

By default participants see one question at a time. To show several together, choose **Add page** in the study builder's add menu, or turn on **Show on one page** in a group's settings. Participants then see all the page's questions stacked on one screen and answer them with a single **Next**. The page's name, if you give it one, shows as its heading, translated like the rest of the study. Pages work in every study mode except voice-to-voice, where the interviewer still asks them one at a time.

- **Question types** — a page can hold Multiple Choice, Rating Scale, Matrix Rating Scale, Matrix Selection, NPS, Star Rating, Number, Date, Email, Short Text, Open Text, Ranking, Matrix Ranking, Allocation, Matrix Allocation, and Message. It can't hold groups or randomizers, and a question on a page can't have a Figma prototype, an embedded website or HTML file, or an AI follow-up. Open Text answers on a page are typed, not recorded.
- **Required questions** — when the participant selects **Next** with a required question unanswered, the page points to each one and scrolls to the first. The footer shows how many required questions are left; optional questions are marked **Optional**.
- **Media** — a question's image, video, or PDF shows inline under it, whatever its layout setting.
- **Going back** — participants can go back to a page, where their answers are kept, and change them. As with a single question, a change that affects branching or which questions show later sends them on from the page again; any other change keeps the answers they gave after it.
- **Logic** — branching runs once the whole page is submitted. See [Logic on a page](/studies/logic#logic-on-a-page).
- **Progress** — the progress bar counts a page as one step.

Answers are saved per question, so results, exports, and transcripts look the same as for questions shown one at a time.

## Open response

### AI Question

The signature Versive question: an open-ended question where the AI interviewer probes with intelligent follow-ups based on what the participant says.

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Limit follow-up questions | Cap the number of AI follow-ups (1–10, default 5 when enabled). Leave off for unlimited. |
| Add instructions | Guidance shown to the AI interviewer for this question (also displayed as a subtitle to participants). |
| Interviewer notes | Private notes that steer the AI's probing — Versive can also suggest these automatically. |
| Duration tracker | Show an on-screen timer with a target response window (`min`/`max` duration). The timer is yellow before the minimum, green in range, and red from the maximum on. |
| Follow-up durations | Separate min/max duration targets for follow-up answers (defaults to 50% of the main question's targets, with a 15-second minimum). |

### Exploratory

A goal-driven, multi-turn AI conversation. Instead of a fixed question, you give the AI a **research goal** and a **turn budget** (default 8 turns), and it explores the topic conversationally until the goal is covered or the budget runs out.

Exploratory questions support the same **duration tracker** settings as AI Questions:

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Enable duration tracker | Show participants an elapsed-time timer for each response. |
| Response duration range (optional) | Set min/max targets in seconds for the opening response. The timer changes color after the participant starts typing or recording: yellow below the minimum, green within range, and red at the maximum. |
| Follow-up response duration (optional) | Override min/max targets for later responses. Each unset target defaults to 50% of the corresponding opening target, with a 15-second minimum. |

The timer resets for each new question or follow-up. It pauses after 30 seconds without an initial response and resumes when the participant types or records; stopping a recording also pauses it. Reaching the maximum shows a reminder to wrap up, without forcing the participant to advance. These targets apply to individual responses, not the entire exploratory conversation.

Targets must be non-negative whole seconds, and the minimum cannot exceed the maximum when both bounds of a response range are explicitly set. A single follow-up override is allowed even if it falls outside the automatic bound; in that case the timer may have no green interval.

Turning the tracker off in the editor clears its duration targets; disabling it through the assistant or MCP preserves them for reuse. Ask the assistant to clear a specific target to remove it, or clear a follow-up override to restore automatic timing. MCP callers can pass `clear_duration_fields` to `edit_question` with a list containing `min_duration`, `max_duration`, `follow_up_min_duration`, or `follow_up_max_duration`; passing `null` for a duration argument keeps its stored value unchanged.

### Open Text

Long-form free text (or voice) input. Supports optional instructions shown to participants, and an optional **character limit** — participants see a live counter and typing stops at the limit.

### Short Text

A brief, single-line text answer. Supports an optional **character limit** with a live counter, a **minimum** number of characters, and an **answer format**.

| Answer format | Accepts |
| --- | --- |
| Phone number | International format: a `+`, the country code and the number (7–15 digits in all; spaces, dashes, dots and parentheses allowed), like `+52 55 1234 5678`. |
| Postal code | The format of the country you pick, from about 180 countries that use postal codes, like `06600` for Mexico or `SW1A 1AA` for the United Kingdom. |
| Numbers only | Digits 0–9, no decimals or signs. |
| Letters only | Letters in any language, plus spaces, hyphens and apostrophes. |
| Website (URL) | A web address such as `example.com` or `https://example.com/page`. |
| Custom pattern | A regular expression the **whole** answer must match (JavaScript syntax), e.g. `[A-Z]{2}[0-9]{4}`. Patterns are limited to 200 characters, and patterns that can match the same text in too many ways are rejected because they can hang the check: use at most 3 repeats (`+`, `*`), a limited number of optional parts and choices, and don't repeat a group that contains one, like `(a+)+` or `(a\|b)+`. |

Add an **example answer** to show participants what you expect; it appears as the placeholder and in the error message (presets have their own default example). Use the **Try it** box in the card to test an answer before publishing. Participants can't continue while a typed answer breaks the format or the minimum; the reason appears under the answer, in the participant's language, once they leave the field. Leading and trailing spaces are ignored. On phones the keyboard matches the format (numeric keypad for numbers and numeric postal codes, phone pad for phone numbers). The rule is checked again on the server, and simulated participants answer in the requested format.

### Message

Not a question — displays information, instructions, or media between questions. Settings: custom button text (default "Sounds good") and an optional **timer** that participants must wait out before continuing.

## Choices & sorting

### Multiple Choice

Single- or multi-select from a list of options.

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Multi-select | Allow choosing more than one option, with an optional **max selections** limit. |
| Display format | Single-select only: show options as the classic list, or collapse long option lists into a **dropdown**. |
| Columns | Show the list in **one**, **two** or **three** columns, or **Automatic** (one column up to 10 options, two up to 20, three above 20). The order reads down each column, then across. Phones always show one column. |
| Searchable | Adds a search box: above the list, or inside the dropdown. Participants type part of an option to find it; case and accents don't matter. Options picked before a search stay picked. |
| Exclusive options | Multi-select only: mark options like "None of the above" as exclusive — picking one clears every other selection, and picking any other option clears it. |
| Include "Other" | Adds a free-text "Other" option, with an optional character limit for the field. |
| Randomize order | Shuffle option order per participant to avoid order bias. |
| Anchor position | With Randomize on: anchor an option so it keeps its place in the list while the other options shuffle, for example "None of these" last or "All of these" first. Each participant's order stays the same when they go back. Set anchors in the default language; translations keep them. "Other" always stays after the list. |

### Image Choice

Participants answer by tapping a picture, and can enlarge a picture first. Use it to compare packaging, logos, or concepts. Each answer has its own picture, and the answer is saved as that answer's text, so logic, quotas, filters, and exports work as they do for Multiple Choice.

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Pictures | One picture per answer, up to 6 (JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP, or BMP). Add alt text for screen readers; without it, screen readers read the answer text. |
| None of these | Always there, always last, with no picture. You can rename it. |
| Randomize order | On by default. Only the pictures shuffle. |
| Anchor position | With Randomize on: anchor a picture so it keeps its place while the other pictures shuffle. Set anchors in the default language. |
| Multi selection | Allow choosing several pictures, with an optional **max selections** limit. None of these clears the others. |
| Include "Other" | Adds a free-text "Other" answer below the pictures. |
| Picture size | Small, Medium, or Large show 4, 3, or 2 columns on a desktop. Phones always show 2 columns. |
| Show answer text | Show each answer's text under its picture. |

To ask why, add an AI Question or Open Text question after it, then choose **Add asset > The picture picked in question N**. That question shows only the picture the participant picked, and the AI interviewer reads that picture's description, which is written when you save the study. If the participant picked None of these or Other, no picture shows. This needs a single-select Image Choice question.

The study editor does not offer Image Choice in voice-only studies.

### Matrix Selection

Participants pick **one answer for each row**, or several with **Multi selection** on: rows are the items, and the answer choices are shared by every row. Use it when the answers are labelled choices rather than a numeric scale (for that, use [Matrix Rating Scale](#matrix-rating-scale)).

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Rows and answers | Each row and each answer label must be unique. You need at least one row and two answers. |
| Multi selection | Participants can pick several answers in each row (checkboxes, with a "Select all that apply in each row" hint). Multi selection always uses the grid. |
| Display format | **Grid** shows a radio button per answer; on phones each row stacks its answers. **Dropdown** gives each row its own dropdown, which keeps long answer text readable without scrolling sideways. Single selection only. |
| Required | Every row needs an answer (at least one with Multi selection). Optional questions can be skipped untouched, or answered for some rows only. |
| Randomize order | Shuffle the rows per participant. Answers keep their order. |

Results show the answer distribution for each row. With Multi selection, each row's percentages are over the participants who answered that row, so they can add up to more than 100%. The CSV export has one column per row holding the chosen answer; with Multi selection it has one column per row and answer (the answer when picked, blank when not), plus an "Unmatched" column per row for answers renamed or deleted after they were picked. A later question can reference the answer for one row with `selectionForItem[index]`; several answers are joined with ", " (see [Referencing earlier answers](#referencing-earlier-answers)). With Multi selection on, logic on a row uses **Includes** and **Does not include** (see [Logic & branching](/studies/logic#conditions)).

### Card Sort

Participants sort cards into categories, which suits information architecture research. Settings: cards, categories, randomize order, **Custom categories** (open or hybrid sort), and **Custom cards**. Paste a list into a card or category field to add one entry per line.

For a closed sort, list your categories and leave **Custom categories** off. For a hybrid sort, list your categories and turn **Custom categories** on. For an open sort, remove every category and turn **Custom categories** on, so participants create and name all of them.

### Ranking

Rank a list of options by preference, via **drag-and-drop** or **dropdown** format. Supports randomized order.

Turn on **Rank top N** to ask for the top choices only, for example the top 3 of 15 vendors. Choose the number (from 1 to the number of options) and whether participants rank **exactly** that many or **up to** that many.

- **Drag and drop:** the ranked options sit in numbered slots on top, and the other options wait in a list below. Participants tap an option to rank it, tap it again to take it back, and drag the handle to reorder. This works the same way on phones.
- **Dropdown:** each option offers ranks 1 to N only.
- Participants can continue once they have ranked N options (or at least one with **up to**), with no rank skipped or repeated. An optional question can still be skipped with nothing ranked.

The answer holds only the ranked options. Results show, for each option, the share of responses that put it in the top N, the share that ranked it 1st, and its average rank among the people who ranked it. An option nobody ranked shows 0%. The CSV export keeps one column per option and leaves an unranked option's cell empty. A later question can quote 1st to Nth place with `optionAtRank` (see [Referencing earlier answers](#referencing-earlier-answers)).

### Matrix Ranking

Rank options across multiple **criteria** (e.g. rank features by importance *and* by satisfaction). Same format options as Ranking.

### Allocation

Distribute a fixed budget of points across options (default total: 100). Options can optionally be pulled from a previous question's answers, and you can enforce the total as a hard limit. With the limit on, options left blank count as 0 (and are saved as 0) once the values add up to the total, so participants don't have to type 0 into the rest. The other rules below still apply: a custom option needs a name and a value, and a failing check against an earlier answer still blocks. Turn on **custom options** to let participants add their own options, each with a name and a value; their values count in the total, and a name that is already in the list is refused. With the limit off, each option is a separate value, and an optional **range** (a Min and a Max) applies to every value, including the options participants add: for example 0 to 100 for a percentage per option, or -100 to 100 for growth that can also be a decline. An empty Min means 0, an empty Max means no maximum. Participants see the range under the options and cannot continue while a value is outside it. With the limit on, every value must be 0 or more; when the total must equal an earlier answer that is negative, every value must be between that total and 0 instead, and when the total must be at most such an answer, every value must be that total or more. The total, or each option, can also be checked against an earlier answer — see [Checks against earlier answers](#checks-against-earlier-answers).

Choose a **Display format**: **Number inputs** (the default, a number box per option) or **Sliders**, one per option with a number box beside it, for questions like "What share of your usage goes to each treatment? (total 100%)". Dragging a slider and typing in its box stay in sync, and a **Total** row under the options shows the running total while there is a total to reach. The sliders add:

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Step | How much a slider moves per step, a whole number. It must divide the range evenly, so a slider can reach its end. Default 1. |
| Show value | Show a number box beside each slider (on by default). Turn it off to show the sliders alone. With a step above 1, the boxes stay when the question has checks against earlier answers or lets participants add options, since those can need a value between steps. |
| Stop at the total | With **Has allocation limit** on: a slider can't go past the amount that's left. Off (the default), the total can go over; it shows in orange until it matches, and the participant can't continue until then. |

With the limit on, every slider runs from 0 to the total. With the limit off, every slider runs from the range's Min to its Max, so the sliders need a Max. Every slider starts at 0 (or at the Min when the range starts above 0), and participants don't have to touch each one: once they move any slider, the rest count at their start value. Options participants add with **custom options** keep a number box. The answer is the same as with number inputs, so logic, quotas, references to earlier answers, results and exports work the same way. The sliders work with the keyboard (arrow keys) and on touch screens; on a phone, each option's name sits above its slider.

Turn on **N/A option** to add a box at the end of each option row, with your own text in **N/A label** (for example "Unable to assess"; empty shows "N/A"). A participant enters a value or ticks the box, never both: ticking it clears and disables that option's field (or its slider). An option answered N/A counts as answered, adds 0 to the total, and skips the range and any check against an earlier answer; with the limit on, the other options must still add up to the total, and when every option is N/A there is nothing left to allocate. Answers store `"N/A"` for that option. Results and exports show your label, and Insights leave N/A out of the averages and show how many participants answered N/A per option.

### Matrix Allocation

A grid of values: **rows** are the things being split, **columns** are scenarios or time points (up to 8), and each column is filled in on its own. With **Has allocation limit** on (the default), every column must add up to the **total allocation** (default 100), and participants see a live total under each column. With the limit off, each cell is a separate value, an optional **range** (a Min and a Max, the Min may be negative) applies to every cell, and columns don't need to add up. An empty Min means 0, an empty Max means no maximum. Participants type numbers with up to two decimal places. With the limit on, cells left blank count as 0 (and are saved as 0) once the column adds up to the total. With the limit off, every cell needs a value. Columns never affect each other. Click a cell in the **N/A cells** grid to mark it not applicable (for example, a product launching in 2027 has no 2026 value): participants see it greyed out as N/A and can't enter a value, it doesn't count toward the column's total, and a column whose cells are all N/A shows only N/A, with no total to reach. Supports randomized row order; columns keep their order. Results show one Allocation-style breakdown per column, leaving N/A cells out of the averages; exports show N/A in those cells.

Turn on **N/A option** to let participants answer a whole row N/A, with your own text in **N/A label** (for example "Unable to assess"; empty shows "N/A"). Each row gets a box at its end; ticking it clears and disables the row in every column. The row's cells then work like N/A cells: they don't count toward any column's total, and answers store `"N/A"` in them. A row whose cells are all N/A cells has no box. Results and exports show your label for these rows (and N/A for the cells you marked), and Insights show how many participants answered each row N/A.

### Conjoint

Choice-based conjoint analysis (CBC): participants repeatedly choose between product concepts assembled from your attributes, revealing how much each attribute drives preference.

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Attributes & levels | The product dimensions being traded off (e.g. Price, Quality, Support), each with 2–5 levels. 3–6 attributes recommended. |
| Concepts per task | How many concepts appear side by side in each choice (2 or 3). |
| Tasks per respondent | How many choice tasks each participant completes (6–12). |
| Include "None" option | Adds a "None of these" choice to every task. |
| Randomization | Shuffle task order and concept order per participant. |

Results include **attribute importance**, **level utilities**, **choice shares**, and design diagnostics — all computed automatically in Insights.

### MaxDiff

Best-worst scaling: you list 6 to 30 items (claims, features, messages), and participants see them in small sets, one set per screen. In each set they pick the item that matters most and the item that matters least. One item can't be both.

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Items | The claims, features or messages to compare, 6 to 30. Each label must be different. |
| Items per set | How many items each screen shows (3, 4 or 5). |
| Sets per participant | How many screens each participant answers (1 to 30). The card shows how many times each item appears and recommends enough sets for each item to appear about 3 times. It must be at least enough to show every item once. |
| Best label / Worst label | The column titles, for example "Most important" and "Least important". Empty shows those defaults in the participant's language. |
| Randomize set order | Shuffle the order of the sets for each participant. |

Versive builds the sets for you: each item appears about equally often, and pairs of items appear together about equally often. Any change to the items, the set size or the number of sets builds new sets; editing a label keeps them. **Regenerate sets** builds new sets on demand. New sets have new ids, so answers collected on the earlier sets no longer count in Insights or the CSV export: settle the design before you launch. Translations change only the labels, never the sets.

Results show each item's **preference share** (the chance it is picked best if every item were shown at once; all shares add up to 100%), how often it was picked best and worst, and a **best-minus-worst score** (-100 to 100), as a ranked chart and table in Insights. With fewer than 50 complete participants, Insights mark the scores as directional; with too few answers for the model, they rank by the best-minus-worst score. On the Results page, a response's transcript shows every set the participant answered, with their best and worst pick marked, and their own score per item. An answer given on sets that were later replaced, and a translated copy of a transcript, keep the plain answer text instead. The CSV export has the best and worst item of each set and each participant's score per item (times picked best minus times picked worst).

MaxDiff questions are created and set up in the study editor: the AI assistant and MCP can't add one or change its items or sets, but they can edit the question text. AI personas skip them in simulations. Participants can't go back into a MaxDiff question once they leave it, but they can step back between its sets.

### Tree Test

Findability testing on a navigation tree. Participants complete tasks by locating the right node in your hierarchy.

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Tree | The nested navigation structure to test. |
| Tasks | Each task has a description and one or more correct answers. |
| Randomize tasks | Shuffle task order. |
| Show task progress | Display progress through tasks (on by default). |
| Max levels | Maximum visible tree depth (default 6). |
| Behavior options | Start expanded, allow skip, allow going back, allow selecting parent nodes, custom instructions. |

## Ratings & feedback

### Rating Scale

A Likert-style scale (default 1–5) with customizable labels for the low, middle, and high points.

Choose a **Display format**: a row of **Buttons** (up to 20 points) or a **Slider** for wider or finer ranges, such as 0–100 % likelihood. The slider adds:

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Step | How much the value changes per move. Decimals are allowed (for example 0.5). It must divide the range evenly, so the slider can reach the max. Default 1. |
| Start position | **None** (default) hides the handle until the participant touches the slider, so every answer is a deliberate choice. **Min** or **Middle** starts the handle there, and the participant can accept that value. That value counts as an answer, so an optional slider with a start position can't be skipped: use **None** to keep it skippable. |
| Show value | Show the selected number above the slider (on by default). |
| N/A option | Add an N/A answer, with your own label such as "Don't know". Picking it disables the slider. |

A required slider needs a value or N/A. The slider works with the keyboard (arrow keys) and on touch screens.

In results, the grid and CSV show the number, or your N/A label. Insights show a histogram, the average without N/A answers, and the N/A count. Logic can test the number with the usual operators, or use **Is N/A**. An N/A answer never matches `=`, `<`, `<=`, `>` or `>=`, but it does match `≠` (N/A is not 5).

### Star Rating

A configurable icon rating — **stars, hearts, or paws** — with an adjustable maximum (default 5).

### NPS

Standard Net Promoter Score (default 0–10) with customizable end labels ("Not at all likely" / "Extremely likely").

### Image Click

Participants click spots on an image. One click makes a first-click test ("Where would you click to start a return?"). Several clicks make a heat map of what draws attention. Add the image with **Add image**; each language can use its own version of it.

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Maximum clicks | The most clicks one participant leaves, 1 to 20. Default: 1. Past the limit, a new click replaces the oldest. |
| Tags | Optional labels participants pick for each click, such as **Like** and **Dislike**, up to 8. |
| Regions | Areas you drag on the image, such as a logo or a price, up to 20. Participants never see them; results show how many participants clicked each one. |
| Required | The participant must click the image at least once. |

Participants tap or click the image to place a mark, then pick a tag if the question has tags. Tapping a mark removes it, or with tags lets them change its tag or remove it. With a keyboard, Enter places a mark in the middle of the image, the arrow keys move it, and Delete removes it.

Insights show a heat map of the clicks (one tag at a time, or all), the share of participants who clicked each region, and the median time to first click, counted from when the image appeared. When a new click replaces an earlier one, the click that stays is the one that counts. Region results use the regions as they are now, so you can draw or move regions after responses come in; click **Update insights** to recount them. The PDF export has the region table and the median time; the heat map stays in Versive. The CSV export has a Clicks column (each click's tag, position and time) and one column per region with 1 (clicked) or 0. Participants never receive the regions: results work out each click's region from its position. Clicks are saved as positions on the image, so phones and computers line up. Insights draw every language's clicks on the default image and count them against its regions, so a translated image must keep the same layout and shape. Image Click can't be a logic source or have quotas, the AI assistant and MCP can create and edit it with its image, click limit and tags (draw regions in the study builder), AI personas skip it in simulations, and voice-only studies don't offer it.

### Matrix Rating Scale

Rate multiple items on the same scale. Supports randomized row order and an optional **N/A** column.

An optional **one selection per column** constraint makes each scale value usable by at most one row — once a row picks a value, it's greyed out for the others (N/A stays shared). Useful for forced-ranking grids. The toggle is only available when every row can still receive a value: at least as many scale points as rows, or the N/A column enabled.

### Text Highlight

Participants highlight words or phrases in a passage and label each highlight with a category, such as **Like** or **Dislike**. Use it to test message, ad, or packaging copy: for example, "Looking at this message, what about it is not believable?"

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Text to highlight | The text participants highlight, up to 5,000 characters. Plain text; line breaks are kept. |
| Categories | The labels for a highlight, 1 to 8, each unique. Each category has its own color. Default: Like and Dislike. |
| Maximum highlights | The most highlights one participant can make, from 1 to 100. Leave it empty for no limit. |
| Required | The participant must make at least one highlight. |

Highlights always start and end on whole words. On a computer, participants drag across the words; on a phone, they tap the first word and then the last. Then they pick a category. Tapping a highlight lets them change its category or remove it, and a new highlight replaces any it overlaps.

Insights show the passage shaded by how many participants highlighted each word, one category at a time, and the 10 phrases highlighted most in each category. The transcript lists each category with its quoted highlights, and the CSV export has one column per category with the quoted phrases. The shading uses the default-language passage. If you edit the passage after responses come in, a highlight is shaded wherever its exact words still appear. Words no longer in the passage, and most highlights from translated interviews, appear in the phrase list but not in the shading; a translated highlight is shaded only when its exact words also appear in the default-language passage, such as a number or a brand name. Text Highlight can be a logic source and a results filter ("highlighted as" a category), and its answers can be piped into later questions. It can't have quotas, and AI personas skip it in simulations.

A Figma prototype navigation task. Participants attempt to complete a task in your interactive prototype while Versive tracks their path.

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Goal node | The Figma frame that counts as task success — completion is detected automatically. |
| Enable recording | Record the participant's prototype session. |

### Website Task

Send participants to a live website or prototype to complete a task.

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| URL & button text | The site to visit and the button label (default "Visit website"). |
| Voice recording | Ask participants to think aloud while browsing (on by default). |
| Embed the site | Show the site inside the study instead of a new tab. Published prototypes from **Figma Make, v0, Lovable, Bolt, Replit, and Framer** are embedded directly — participants use the real app in-page. Other websites open in a secure virtual browser. |

Redirect the participant to an external URL (e.g. a partner survey or booking page).

### Media Upload

### Email

Capture an email address. An answer must look like `name@example.com` before the participant can continue; an optional Email question can still be skipped empty.

### Number

Numeric input, with an optional min/max range validation. The number can also be checked against an earlier answer — see [Checks against earlier answers](#checks-against-earlier-answers).

Turn on **N/A option** to add a box under the field, with your own text in **N/A label** (for example "I don't use this product"; empty shows "N/A"). A participant types a number or ticks the box, never both: ticking it clears and disables the field. The range and the checks apply only to a typed number, and a check that compares with an N/A answer is skipped. Answers store `"N/A"`; results, exports and answer references show your label. Insights count N/A answers on their own row, outside the total and the percentages. Logic can test the answer with **Is N/A** and **Is not N/A**.

### Date

Structured date entry with month/day selectors and a typed four-digit year. Full date is the default; authors can instead collect month & year or year only and choose MDY, DMY, or YMD display order. Optional earliest/latest limits restrict which dates participants can submit. Answers remain stored in sortable ISO partial-date format: `YYYY-MM-DD` for full dates, `YYYY-MM` for month and year, and `YYYY` for year only. Storage is independent of display order.

Turn on **N/A option** to add a box under the date fields, with your own text in **N/A label** (empty shows "N/A"). Ticking it clears and disables the fields; the earliest/latest limits apply only to a picked date. Answers store `"N/A"`; results and exports show your label, and Insights count N/A answers on their own row.

### Calendly

External Calendly booking — share a scheduling link (e.g. for a follow-up session) inside the study.

## AI follow-ups on closed questions

After a participant answers a closed question, the AI interviewer can ask why, so the reasons sit next to the number. Turn on **AI follow-up** on Multiple Choice, Rating Scale, Star Rating, NPS, Ranking, Matrix Rating Scale, Matrix Selection, Matrix Ranking, Matrix Allocation or Text Highlight.

| Setting | Description |
|---|---|
| Instructions | What the interviewer should find out. |
| Limit follow-up questions | Cap the follow-ups for this question at 1, 2 or 3. Leave off and the interviewer decides when it has enough. |
| Only for some answers | Ask only for the answers you choose, such as NPS detractors. Ranking and Matrix Ranking use positions, such as first and last. Matrix Allocation asks about the rows with the most and the fewest points in each column. Text Highlight uses categories, and the interviewer asks about the phrases highlighted with them. |

The follow-up replaces the question on screen, and participants answer in the study's response mode. If the answer sends the participant out of the study through logic or a full quota, or an optional question is skipped, there is no follow-up. Logic and quotas always use the closed answer, so a follow-up never changes where the participant goes next.

Follow-ups appear under the question in the transcript and results grid, and in a "follow-up" column in the CSV. They aren't available on surveys.

## Required and request response

A required question can't be skipped. When **Required** is off, you can turn on **Request response**: if the participant tries to continue without answering, they see one reminder ("You haven't answered this question…") and the button changes to **Skip anyway**; the second click moves on. A skipped request-response question is stored as an empty answer, like any skipped optional question. Format rules still apply to anything the participant does type. **Ranking** questions don't offer Request response. Without **Rank top N** they are never left empty: drag and drop starts with an order, and dropdowns need every item ranked.

Request response works the same in surveys and in AI-moderated interviews (text and voice), since the participant answers structured questions on screen in both.

## Referencing earlier answers

Questions can reference earlier answers with **mentions** — type `@` in the question editor to pipe a previous answer into the question text. See [Reusing earlier answers](/studies/logic#reusing-earlier-answers-mentions).

## Checks against earlier answers

A Number or Allocation question can require its answer to compare with an earlier answer — for example, "patients who started treatment this year" must be at most "patients under your care", or an age-group allocation must total exactly that number. Open the question card and turn on **Checks against earlier answers**, then pick the earlier question (a Number question, or an Allocation question and one of its options) and the comparison: less than, less than or equal to, equal to, greater than or equal to, or greater than.

| Question | What receives the checks |
| --- | --- |
| Number | The typed number. |
| Allocation, **By total allocation** | The total. While the total has a check, **Has allocation limit** is off; an "equal to" check takes its place, so options left blank count as 0 once the values add up to the earlier answer, and each value must be between 0 and the earlier answer (the card's **Range** does not apply). |
| Allocation, **By allocation option** | One option of this question per check (e.g. "2025" must be at least "4Q25"). Options without a check stay free. |

A Number answer, and each Allocation option, can carry several checks; the Allocation total carries one. Several checks on one answer are joined with "and": every check must pass. The builder does not stop you from writing checks that no answer can satisfy, such as "equal to Q1" together with "less than Q1"; a participant who meets such a pair is stuck on the question, so check each pair against the answers you expect.

Participants see the check as a hint ("Enter a number 78 or less"; for a total with an "equal to" or "less than or equal to" check, "78 remaining") and cannot continue while a typed answer fails a check (an optional question can still be left blank). With the limit on, once the values add up to the total, an Allocation option left blank counts as 0 and is checked as 0. A check that fails prints one line under the answer, in the participant's language, such as "The number must be at most 100." or "The total must be at least 1200."; an "equal to" or "less than or equal to" check on an Allocation total shows the remaining count instead. If the earlier question was skipped, or answered with something other than a number, the check does not apply — the card warns you when the earlier question is not required. When a participant goes back and changes an earlier answer that a check reads, the later answers stay; the later question is checked again against the new value when the participant reaches it, and it blocks until the answer fits.

Renaming an option keeps its checks; deleting one drops them. If the earlier question is deleted, changes type, or ends up after the question it checks (including through randomization), the card flags the check so you can fix it. To also show the earlier value in the question text, add a [mention](#referencing-earlier-answers). Simulated interviews respect these checks too.
